How I work.

Simple on purpose.

  1. 01
    Tell me what you need

    No jargon required. Describe the problem or the idea and I'll take it from there.

  2. 02
    I scope it honestly

    I'll tell you straight what it actually takes to build, and if I'm not the right person for it, I'll say so.

  3. 03
    I build and ship it

    I own the whole build myself, frontend to backend, until it's live and working, not handed off half done.

  4. 04
    You test it, I fix what's off

    You try it for real before it's called finished. If something's not right, I fix it, not argue about it.

  5. 05
    I stick around after launch

    I don't disappear the moment it ships. If something breaks or needs a small change later, you can still reach me.

    Terms

    These are the terms I work under on any paid engagement. They exist so both sides know what to expect going in, and so anything that goes wrong gets handled fairly rather than argued about after the fact. Starting a paid project means both sides have agreed to them.

    1. Work environment

    I work with people who communicate respectfully. Specifically:

    1. No yelling, no talking down to me, and no unreasonable pressure or last-minute demands.
    2. If a conversation turns toxic, I may end the project immediately and walk away.
    3. Payment following an early end under this clause is settled under clause 5, based on work actually completed, not a blanket refund or a full forfeit.
    Example: a client starts yelling on a call or sending demanding, disrespectful messages. I end the engagement and settle payment for whatever was already built.

    2. Scope

    Before I write a line of code, we agree in writing on what's actually being built. Specifically:

    1. Scope changes are normal and expected, but they get discussed and priced before I build them.
    2. Work outside the agreed scope is not assumed to be included for free.
    3. Scope changes can go the other way too. In rare cases, if something originally quoted turns out to be unnecessary or gets cut before I build it, the price can be adjusted down from the original quote, though this is the exception rather than the rule.
    Example: you ask for a login system partway through a project that didn't originally include one. That's a scope change, it gets a price and timeline before I start it, not silently folded into the original quote. If instead you decide a feature I quoted for isn't needed after all and I haven't built it yet, the price can come down to reflect that.

    3. Deadlines

    Timelines are set to be realistic, not optimistic. Specifically:

    1. If something is going to be late, I'll say so as soon as I know, not on the deadline itself.
    2. In return, I expect the same honesty about your side, assets, feedback, or access I need from you to keep the project moving.
    Example: I said Friday and it's actually going to be Monday. You hear that from me by Wednesday, not Friday afternoon.

    4. Communication

    How and how fast I respond depends on the channel. Specifically:

    1. Discord is my preferred channel. I check it constantly through the day, so it's the fastest way to reach me.
    2. Email works too, but responses may take noticeably longer since I check it far less often.
    3. Regardless of channel, you should expect a reply within about a day under normal circumstances.
    Example: a Discord message sent on a Tuesday usually gets a same-day reply. The same message sent by email might not get a response until the next day.

    5. Payment

    Money is agreed on before work starts, not figured out after. Specifically:

    1. Price and payment structure are agreed upfront, in writing, before work starts.
    2. For larger projects, I may ask for a portion upfront; the remainder is due on delivery, not whenever it's convenient.
    3. If a project ends early because these terms are violated, payment is worked out based on how much was actually completed at that point, not a fixed all-or-nothing refund.
    Example: a $600 project that's roughly half delivered when it ends early under these terms settles around $300, not $0 and not the full $600.

    6. Enforcement

    These terms exist to protect both sides, not to force an outcome. Specifically:

    1. If something in clauses 1 through 5 is technically triggered, I can choose to invoke it right away, or choose to keep the project going if I think it's still workable.
    2. Letting something go once doesn't waive my right to invoke that same term later if it happens again.
    Example: a deadline slips once and I decide it's not worth ending things over, so I just keep going. If it becomes a pattern, I can still invoke clause 3 later even though I let the first one slide.
